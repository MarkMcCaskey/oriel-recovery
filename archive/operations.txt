LINDEN UNIVERSITY · SPACE SYSTEMS LABORATORY
ORIEL-1 / Ground operations handover / 2031-04-07

MISSION
A 1U university spacecraft launched in 2014. Engineering records stopped
passing acceptance checks in 2016. Later short contacts reported a carrier,
link beacons, and some command replies. No in-orbit recovery is recorded.
The engineering team no longer exists as an organized group.

Your workstation contains the surviving archive and a reconstructed local
bench. Establish what still works, make a diagnosis you can defend, and
attempt to restore useful engineering data and sustained operation.

STATION BASICS
'files' lists the archive and your workspace. 'cat' reads text. Addresses
accept decimal or 0x-prefixed hexadecimal. Raw bytes use plain hex pairs.
'help' lists the tools. 'track' and 'passes' report predicted geometry.
'next' advances to the next acquisition of signal plus five seconds.
If already in contact, 'next' deliberately goes to the following pass.
'wait 10' advances both spacecraft and station by ten simulated seconds.
The clock never follows wall time. Reading files consumes no contact time.

Commands enter a station transmit queue. They are not delivered by typing
them. Advance time, inspect 'rx' and 'log', and account for acknowledgements.
The line 'SENT' means transmission finished, not that the receiver accepted it.
Software replies have their own sequence numbers, unrelated to uplink IDs.

Frames are indivisible at the receiver. Loss of signal may interrupt the
current frame; completed earlier frames remain delivered. Unsent frames
stay in the station queue for the next pass. 'cancel' clears only unsent
frames. There are no automatic retries. Retry carefully: commands may have
executed even if their acknowledgement was lost. Repeating a RAM write of
the same bytes is idempotent; repeating a service need not be.

The spacecraft continues executing and transmitting below the horizon.
You will not see those transmissions. Independent link beacons are not
proof of a functioning flight application. Radio buffers have finite depth.

WORKSPACE
Browser: open the Workbench tab to edit assembly or import/export files.
CLI: 'import path [workspace-name]' and 'export name path' move local files.
Paths containing spaces can be handled by placing files in a simpler path.
'asm source.asm BASE output.bin' produces a binary and a symbol listing.
'upload file.bin ADDRESS' queues chunks, but does not advance the clock.
The upload tool uses the documented RAM-load command; it cannot bypass its
flight software restrictions. All file addresses and lengths are checked.

'lab help' describes the independent bench. It starts from flight.bin,
with nominal devices. You can load images, step instructions, set a PC or
breakpoint, inspect memory, and introduce your own hardware hypotheses.
That bench is a reconstruction, not a live debugger attached to ORIEL-1.

RECORD KEEPING
The CLI saves to .local/station.session after each command. Browser sessions
are stored locally in this browser. Export a session to transfer between
CLI and browser. A running transmission, memory contents, clock, files,
radio noise state and local bench are preserved. Ground traffic history is
bounded to the most recent 4096 records; export your notes independently.

Normal mode: 'snapshot NAME' saves one of eight investigation checkpoints;
'restore NAME' returns to it. 'new' starts a new investigation.
Hardcore mode disables checkpoints, restore, and restart commands. Normal
persistence still works. This is an honor-system constraint on your local
machine, not anti-cheat software. Keep a separate session path per campaign.

Success must be judged from engineering evidence, including fresh data,
plausible measurements, continuing sample production, and behavior across
loss of contact and reset. There is no hidden score or victory password.
